# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to build something too.

## What is expected

- Criticise work, not people. "This breaks on Windows because the path separator
  differs" is useful; "this is careless" is not.
- Accept that other people have different constraints: different hardware,
  different budgets, different amounts of time, different first languages.
- When you are wrong, say so and move on. Nobody is keeping score.
- Give the context a newcomer needs rather than the answer an expert would want.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, or sustained hostility, in issues, pull requests, commits,
  or anywhere else this project happens.
- Personal attacks, and demeaning remarks about who someone is, including
  ethnicity, nationality, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability,
  age, religion, or appearance.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussion, or wearing people down through repetition.

## Scope

This applies in every space the project uses: the repository, its issues and
pull requests, and any channel where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer, at the contact address on
[github.com/hugothomel](https://github.com/hugothomel). If you would rather not
contact anyone here at all, use GitHub's own
[report abuse](https://docs.github.com/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
flow. Reports are kept confidential and you will get a reply.

Do not open a public issue about a conduct problem. It escalates the situation
for the person who was harmed.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will do what the situation calls for, in proportion: a private word,
a public correction, edited or removed content, a temporary block, or a permanent
ban. Maintainers who do not hold to this document should expect the same
treatment as anyone else.
